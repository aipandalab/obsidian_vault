I hit the fence with a terrible crash. The car stoped in broken wooden boards. My hand stayed tight on the wheel. For a second, I could not move. The engine made a low sound. I quickly turned the key and stopped the engine. Smoke was not coming from the car. Then I checked my arms and legs. Nothing hurt, but I felt shaken. I opened the door very slowly. My knees felt weak under me. I stepped outside and looked around. The front hood was badly dented. The wooden fence was broken open. I whispered, "What did I do?" Then I remembered the birthday cake. I had left the cake inside the car.  I hurried back to the passenger seat.  The cake box was on its side. I picked it up very carefully. The box was closed, and the cake looked safe. I held it against my chest. Then my grandson ran outside fast. He wore socks, but no shoes. "Grandpa, are you hurt?" he shouted. "I am okay," I told him. "But the fence looks terrible." My son came out with tools. My daughter-in-law came with her phone. Everyone stared at the broken fence. Then everyone stared at my car. I pointed to the cake box. "At least the birthday cake survived." My granddaughter came out and smiled. She hugged me before checking the cake. That made me feel much better. We called a repair shop nearby. My son checked the car's engine. Later, we fixed the fence together. Now I always check both pedals.
