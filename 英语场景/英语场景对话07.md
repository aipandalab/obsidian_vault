## 1. You Pushed Me!
> A: Excuse me! Can't you see me standing here?
> B: Oh! What happened, ma'am? **ma'am**：名词，意为“女士 / 太太”，是对成年女性较为尊称的称呼（常见于服务或公共场所）。
> A: Don't you have any manners? _manner_ 通常用复数形式 _manners_，指社会交往中的礼貌、规矩或教养。
> B: Excuse me? What are you talking about?
> A: You just pushed me. Why did you do that?
> B: Huh? I pushed you?
> A: Of course you did. Who else was it? 
> B: Oh, I'm very sorry if I did.
> A: If you did? You're standing right behind me.
> B: I know, but I didn't push you on purpose. **on purpose**：非常高频的介词短语，意为“故意地 / 有意地”。其反义词是 _by accident_ 或 _by mistake_（无意中、不小心）。
> A: Then how did I suddenly move forward? 
> B: Someone bumped into me from behind.
> A: And then you bumped into me?
> B: Yes. That's exactly what happened.
> A: You could have said sorry immediately. 
> B: I didn't realize I had touched you.
> A: Well, you pushed me pretty hard.
> B: I understand. I'm sorry about that.
> A: Just be careful. This line is already crowded. **crowded**：形容词，意为“拥挤的 / 人满为患的”。
> B: I'm trying, but people keep moving forward.
> A: Everyone is in a hurry today.
> B: Yes, and the line is barely moving. **barely**：副词，意为“几乎不 / 勉强”，在这里修饰动词 _moving_，表示队伍挪动得极其缓慢。
> A: That doesn't mean people can keep pushing.
> B: I agree. But please don't blame me for everything.但是请不要把所有事情都怪在我头上。 **blame someone for something**：非常地道的固定搭配，意为“因某事而责怪某人 / 把……归咎于某人”。
> A: I'm blaming you because you were right behind me.
> B: And I'm telling you someone pushed me first.
> A: Hmm... are you sure you're not making an excuse? **make an excuse**：固定搭配，意为“找借口 / 找理由搪塞”
> B: No, I'm not. Look behind me. There's hardly any space.**hardly any**：非常地道的固定搭配，意为“几乎没有 / 几乎全无”。_hardly_ 带有否定含义，常用于修饰可数名词或不可数名词。
> A: Oh... I can see that now.
> B: See? Everyone is standing too close together.
> A: All right. Maybe I got angry too quickly.
> B: It's okay. I would be annoyed too. **annoyed**：形容词，意为“烦恼的 / 恼怒的 / 生气的”
> A: Sorry for speaking to you like that.
> B: No problem. And I'm sorry for bumping into you. **bump into someone**：意为“撞到某人”，在这里指之前的身体意外碰撞。
> A: Okay. Let's just hope nobody pushes us again.


## 2. That's My Seat
> A: Excuse me. I think you're sitting in my seat.
> B: Your seat? No, I don't think so.
> A: Yes, It is. My ticket says seat 18.
> B: That's strange.  Mine says 18 too. **That's strange**：常用口语，意为“这真奇怪 / 挺蹊跷的”
> A: Are you sure you checked it properly?
> B: Of course, I did. I'm not sitting here for no reason.
> A: Well, I booked this seat yesterday.
> B: And I bought my ticket this morning.
> A: Then maybe you're on the wrong bus.
> B: What? Why would I be on the wrong bus?
> A: Because we can't both have the same seat.
> B: I know that. Let me check my ticket again.
> A: Please do. People are waiting behind me.
> B: Hmm.. It says seat 18, right here.
> A: Can I see the bus number too?
> B: Sure. Here, take a look.
> A: Wait... your bus number is 46.
> B: Yes. Isn't this bus 46?
> A: No. This is bus 64.
> B: Oh, no. Are you serious? 哦，不会吧。你是认真的吗？ / 你没跟我开玩笑吧？
> A: Yes. Look at the screen in front.
> B: How did I get on the wrong bus?
> A: Maybe you were in a hurry.
> B: I was. I saw the number and jumped on.
> A: I think you mixed up 46 and 64. **mix up...**：非常实用的高频动词短语，意为“把……弄混 / 混淆 / 搞错”。常用于两件相似的事物、数字或人被张冠李戴的情况。
> B: Wow. That's embarrassing. **embarrassing**：形容词，意为“尴尬的 / 令人窘迫的”。用来形容因自己的疏忽而感到不好意思的场景。
> A: At least we found the problem.
> B: Yes, but now I might miss my bus.
> A: You should ask the driver before we leave.
> B: Good idea. I'll go right now.
> A: And I'm sorry. I thought you took my seat.
> B: That's okay. I was completely confused too. **confused**：形容词，意为“困惑的 / 不解的 / 糊涂的”。
> A: I hope your bus is still there.
> B: Me too. Thanks for checking my ticket.
> A: No problem. You'd better hurry!


## 3. That's My Cart...
> A: Excuse me! Where are you going with my cart?
> B: Your cart? What do you mean?
> A: That's my shopping cart. Please give it back.
> B: Wait a minute. I thought this was mine.
> A: How could you think that? My groceries are inside it.
> B: I don't look inside. I just grabbed the cart. **grab**：动词，意为“抓取 / 顺手拿来 / 抢先”。在这里 _just grabbed_ 表示“我刚才只是随便拉过来了”。
> A: You should look before taking someone's things.
> B: Okay, calm down. It was just a mistake.
> A: A mistake? You were walking away with all my groceries.
> B: I wasn't stealing them. I picked up the wrong cart. **wasn't stealing**：过去进行时的否定形式，表示“当时并不是在偷（某物）”
> A: Then where is your cart?
> B: It was right here a minute ago.
> A: Well, this one definitely belongs to me.
> B: Alright. Let me check what's inside.
> A: Go ahead. Those are all my things.
> B: Hmm... milk, bread, apples, and coffee.
> A: Exactly. I put them there myself.
> B: You're right. These aren't my groceries.
> A: See? I told you it was my cart.
> B: I know. I'm sorry. Our carts look exactly the same.
> A: All shopping carts look the same.
> B: Yes, that didn't sounds like a good excuse.
> A: No, it really didn't.
> B: But I honestly didn't mean to take your stuff. - **mean to do something**：固定搭配，意为“故意做某事 / 有意图去做……”**。在句型中常用否定形式 _didn't mean to..._，意为**“不是故意的 / 无意做某事”，在日常道歉时非常高频。**stuff**：名词，意为“东西 / 物品 / 玩意儿”。在口语中是个泛指各类物品的高频词。
> A: Fine. Just give me my cart back.
> B: Of course. Here you go.
> A: Thank you. But where did you leave yours? 那你把你的（购物车）留在哪儿了呢？
> B: That's what I'm trying to figure out. 这正是我在努力想明白/琢磨的事情。**figure out**：非常实用的高频动词短语，意为“弄清楚、想明白、琢磨透”。
> A: Wait. Is that your cart near the next counter? 靠近下一个柜台的那辆是你的购物车吗？
> B: Oh! Yes, that's mine.
> A: And it has a big bag of rice inside it.
> B: I can't believe I didn't notice that. 我真不敢相信自己竟然没注意到这一点。
> A: You must be really distracted today. **distracted**：形容词，意为“注意力不集中的 / 心烦意乱的 / 分心的”。
> B: Apparently I am. Sorry again for the confusion. **apparently**：副词，意为“显然 / 看来 / 听说”。**confusion**：名词，意为“混乱、混乱状态、误解”。
> A: It's okay. Just check the cart next time.
> B: Trust me, after this, I definitely will.


## 4. I was here first...
> A: Excuse me! What are you doing?
> B: Sorry? Are you talking to me?
> A: Yes. I was standing here first.
> B: Oh, I'm not trying to take your place.
> A: Then why did you step in front of me? **step in front of someone**：固定搭配，意为“走到某人前面 / 插到某人前面”。
> B: I just nee to ask the cashier(收银员) something.
> A: We're all waiting to talk to the cashier.
> B: I know, but it'll only take a second.
> A: Everyone could say the same thing. **Everyone could say...**：意为“每个人都可以说……”。
> B: Look, I'm not trying to cut in line. - **cut in line**：非常地道的固定搭配，意为“插队 / 挤塞在队伍前面”（英式英语中也常用 _jump the queue_）。**try to do something**：意为“试图/尝试做某事”。否定形式 _not trying to..._ 表示“并非故意/没有企图去做某事”，常用于日常辩解或化解误会。
> A: It certainly looks like you are. （你现在的样子）看起来确实是这样。
> B: My phone is on the counter. I left it there.
> A: Your phone? How did you leave it there?
> B: I ordered my coffee five minutes ago.
> A: Wait... you already ordered?
> B: Yes. I was sitting at that table.
> A: Then why did you come back here?
> B: I realized my phone was missing.
> A: And you think you left it at the counter?
> B: I remember putting it down when I paid.
> A: Oh... I thought you were skipping the line. 我还以为你是在插队呢
> B: I understand, but you didn't even let me explain.
> A: Well, you suddenly walked right in front of me.
> B: You're right. I should have said something first.
> A: Okay. Go ahead and check for your phone.
> B: Thanks. I'll be quick.
> A: Did you find it?
> B: No. It's not on the counter.
> A: Are you sure you left it there?
> B: I was sure... until now.
> A: Have you checked your pockets?
> B: Of course I have. Wait a second...
> A: What happened?
> B: Oh, wow. It's in my jacket pocket.
> A: Seriously? It was with you the whole time? 真的吗？ / 不是吧？ / 有没有搞错？**seriously**：副词，意为“严肃地、认真地”**；但在日常口语（特别是单独作为感叹句）时，常用来表示**吃惊、难以置信、无奈或无语的情绪，相当于中文里的“不会吧”、“真的假的”。
> B: Now I'm the one who should be embarrassed.  **I'm the one who...**：强调句型，意为“我才是那个……的人”。在这里用来突出此时此刻尴尬的人变成了自己。
> A: Well, at least you found it.
> B: And I'm sorry for causing all that confusion.
> A: It's okay. Just don't lose your coffee too!


## 5. Turn off the music...
> A: Excuse me. Can you turn that music down?
> B: What music? What are you talking about? 
> A: That loud music. It's bee playing for about almost an hour.
> B: Wait. You think it's coming from my apartment?
> A: Of course. You live right next to me.
> B: Yes, but I'm not playing any music.
> A: Then why can I hear it through my wall?
> B: I have no idea, but it's definitely not mine. **I have no idea**：非常高频的口语表达，意为“我不知道 / 我毫无头绪”。比简单的 _I don't know_ 语气更地道，常用于对突发状况或他人质问表示茫然。
> A: Are you sure? The sound is really loud.
> B: Lisa, I just got home five minutes ago. 
> A: Five minutes ago? I heard your door earlier.
> B: That wasn't my door. I wasn't even home. 
> A: Hmm... then where is the music coming from?
> B: Let's listen for a second.
> A: There! Can you hear it now?
> B: Yeah, I can. But it sounds like it's coming from upstairs.
> A: Upstairs? I thought it was coming through your wall.
> B: Sound travels strangely in this building.
> A: Well, somebody needs to turn it down.
> B: I agree. It's pretty loud.
> A: I couldn't even hear my TV.
> B: Why didn't you call the building manager?
> A: I thought talking to you would be faster.
> B: And you came straight here to blame me? **blame**：动词，意为“责备 / 归咎于 / 指责”。
> A: Well... yes. I was pretty sure it was you.
> B: You could have asked me first.
> A: Okay, you're right. I was a little rude.
> B: A little? You almost knocked my door down.
> A: Oh, come on. I didn't knock that hard.
> B: I'm kidding. Don't worry about it. **I'm kidding**：极其高频的口语表达，意为“我在开玩笑 / 我逗你玩呢”，用来缓和刚才略显紧绷的对话气氛。
> A: So, should we go upstairs and ask them?
> B: Sure. But this time, let's ask before accusing anyone. **accuse someone**：动词，意为“指责、控告某人”。
> A: Fine. You can do the talking.
> B: Why me? You're the brave one here.
> A: I already argued with the wrong person once tonight. 
> B: Fair enough. I'll handle this one.
> A: Thanks. And Mark... sorry about earlier.
> B: No problem. Now let's find that music before we both lose our minds. **lose one's mind**：经典习惯用语，意为“失去理智、发疯、情绪失控”。


## 6. You Took My Phone!
> A: Excuse me! Is that my phone in your hand?
> B: Your phone? No, this is mine.
> A: I left my phone right here, and now it's gone.
> B: Okay, but what does that have to do with me? **what does that have to do with me?**：非常地道的经典交际句型，意为“这跟我有什么关系？ / 关我什么事？”。
> A: You were standing next to this bench(长凳).
> B: So? That doesn't mean I took your phone.
> A: Yours looks exactly like mine.
> B: Lots of people have the same phone.
> A: Can I see it for a second?
> B: No. Why should I give you my phone?
> A: I want to make sure it isn't mine.
> B: You can't just accuse me like that.
> A: I'm not accusing you. I'm asking you to show me.
> B: You just asked if I took your phone.
> A: Because it disappeared while you were standing here.
> B: I came here thirty seconds ago.
> A: Then prove it. Unlock that phone. **prove it**：经典短语，意为“证明它 / 用证据证明”。
> B: Fine. Look. It's already unlocked.
> A: Wait... that's definitely not my screen.
> B: Exactly. Because this is my phone.
> A: Okay... then where did mine go?
> B: Did you check your gym bag?
> A: Yes. I already checked it twice.
> B: What about under the bench?
> A: I looked there too.
> B: Maybe you left it near one of the machines.
> A: No, I remember putting it right here. **remember doing something**：经典语法结构，意为“记得曾经做过某事”（动作已经发生过）。与之相对的 _remember to do sth._ 则是指“记得去做某事（还没做）”。
> B: Wait. What's that under your towel?
> A: Under my towel? There's nothing.
> B: Lift it up and check.
> A: Oh... you've got to be kidding me. **you've got to be kidding me**（也常说 _you've got to be kidding_）：非常高频的地道口语，意为“你是在逗我吧 / 怎么会这样 / 简直难以置信”。通常在遇到倒霉事、荒谬的情况或被气得无语时脱口而出。
> B: Is that your phone?
> A: Yes. It was under the towel the whole time.
> B: And you thought I stole it.
> A: I'm really sorry. I shouldn't have accused you.
> B: Next time, check under the towel before blaming the guy next to you. 在怪罪你身边的那个家伙之前……
> A: Fair enough. I definitely deserved that. **deserve**：动词，意为“应得、应受（奖励或惩罚）”。_deserved that_ 在这里常用于口语中，表示“某人遭遇了不好的事，但回想起来确实是自己先前种下的因、咎由自取”。



## 7. You Hit My Car!
> A: Excuse me! Did you just hit my car?
> B: What? No. Why would you think that?
> A: Because there's a big scratch on my door. **scratch**：名词，意为“划痕、刮痕、擦伤”。在此处指车辆外壳或门上被刮蹭出的痕迹。
> B: Okay, but I didn't do it.
> A: Your car is parked right next to mine.
> B: That doesn't mean I hit your car.
> A: Were you here when I arrived?
> B: No. I got here about ten minutes ago.
> A: And you didn't touch my car at all?
> B: No. I was careful when I opened my door.
> A: Look how close your car is to mine.
> B: I can see that, but there's still enough space.
> A: This scratch wasn't here this morning.
> B: I'm sorry, but that has nothing to do with me. **have nothing to do with something/someone**：超级高频且实用的地道句型，意为“与……毫无关系 / 跟我没有半毛钱关系”。常用于撇清嫌疑或拒绝背锅。
> A: How can you be so sure? 
> B: Because my door doesn't even reach that part of your car.
> A: What do you mean?
> B: Open my door slowly and look.
> A: Fine. Let's check it.
> B: See? My door would hit much lower. 我的车门（开的时候）撞到的位置要低得多。
> A: Hmm... you're right about that.
> B: So maybe someone else did it.
> A: But your car was the only one beside mine.
> B: Are you sure about that?
> A: Well... it was empty when I came back.
> B: A different car could have been here earlier.
> A: I didn't think about that.
> B: Wait. There's security camera over there.
> A: Oh! I didn't notice it.
> B: We can ask security to check the recording.
> A: Yes. Then we'll know what happened.
> B: Good. Because I don't want you thinking I damaged your car.
> A: I'm sorry. I got angry when I saw the scratch.
> B: I understand. I'd be upset too. 我理解。换作是我，我也会很难过/生气的。**I'd be upset too**：_I'd_ 是 _I would_ 的缩写，属于虚拟语气在日常口语中的常见表达。_upset_ 意为“心烦的、沮丧的、生气的”。这句话展现了男主角的同理心，用“换位思考”来缓和紧张的矛盾气氛。
> A: Wait... I just remembered something.
> B: What?
> A: My sister borrowed my car yesterday.
> B: Maybe you should call her before we call security.
> A: Yeah... I think I owe you another apology.
> B: Call your sister first. Then we'll solve the mystery. **solve the mystery**：意为“解开谜团、查明真相”。


## 8. Did You Take My Bag?
> A: Excuse me. Where are you going with my bag?
> B: Your bag? This is my suitcase.
> A: No, it isn't. I've been waiting for that one. 我（等这个行李）已经等了好久了。
> B: Well, I've been waiting for mine too.
> A: Then you picked up the wrong bag.
> B: I don't think so. This looks exactly like mine.
> A: It looks exactly like mine too.
> B: Okay, so we have similar suitcases.
> A: Similar? That one even has a red ribbon on it.
> B: Yes. I put a red ribbon on mine.
> A: You're joking. I did the same thing. **You're joking**（也常说 _You've got to be kidding_）：非常地道的口语表达，意为“你开玩笑吧 / 不是吧”。当听到一件出乎意料、巧合得离谱的事情时常这样感叹。
> B: Well, that makes this a little confusing. **make something a little confusing**：意为“让某事变得有点令人费解”。
> A: A little? You were about to leave with it. **be about to do something**：极其高频的固定句型，意为“正要 / 刚打算去做某事”（表示动作马上就要发生）。
> B: Because I thought it was mine.
> A: Can you check the name tag, please?
> B: Sure. Let me look.
> A: Well? Whose name is on it?
> B: Hmm... I can't find a name tag.
> A: What? I definitely put one on my bag.
> B: Maybe it came off during the flight.
> A: Don't open the suitcase.
> B: I wasn't going to open it.
> A: Then how are we going to know whose it is? **Then** + **how are we going to know** + **whose it is?**  那么 + 我们怎么才能知道 + 它是谁的？**间接疑问句（embedded question）**，所以语序必须是：**whose it is** ✅ 而不是： **whose is it** ❌（放在这个句子里面不对）如果单独直接问，可以说： **Whose is it?** 这是谁的？
> B: Wait. Do you have a photo of your suitcase?
> A: Yes, I took one before I left home.
> B: Great. Check for something different.
> A: Mine has a small white mark near the bottom.
> B: Okay. Let's look.
> A: There! See that white mark?
> B: Oh... then I guess this really is yours.
> A: I told you it was my suitcase.
> B: I'm sorry. I honestly thought it was mine.
> A: It's okay. At least we checked before you left. 好在我们趁你离开前检查了一下。
> B: Wait... Another black suitcase is coming.
> A: And it has a red ribbon too!
> B: Let me check it. Yes! That one is mine.
> A: We really bought the exact same suitcase.
> B: Apparently we have the same taste. **apparently**：副词，意为“显然、看来、貌似”。常用于根据眼前的现象推测并感叹某种情况。
> A: Next time, I'm using a bright yellow ribbon.
> B: Good idea. And I'm buying a completely different suitcase.


## 9. Who Are You to Stop Me? 
你算老几凭什么拦着我？ / 你凭什么阻止我？
> A: Excuse me! Where are you going?
> B: Inside. Could you move, please?
> A: No. You can't just walk in like that.
> B: What? Who are you to stop me? **Who are you to do something?**：情绪张力十足的愤怒质问句型，意为“你算什么身份凭什么来管我/阻止我”。用来反驳对方的干涉。
> A: I live here, and I've never seen you before.
> B: So? You don't know everyone who lives here.
> A: Maybe not, but you didn't use a key.
> B: Because you already opened the door.
> A: That doesn't mean you can follow me inside.
> B: I'm not following you. I'm going to an apartment.
> A: Then use the intercom(对讲机) and call the person.
> B: Seriously? You're not going to let me in?
> A: Not until I know why you're here.
> B: I don't have to explain myself to you.
> A: Then I don't have to hold the door for you. 我可没义务/用不着为你扶着门。
> B: Fine. I'm here to see my brother.
> A: What apartment does he live in?
> B: Apartment 304. His name is Daniel.
> A: Daniel? I know him. He lives upstairs.
> B: Great. Then can I go inside now?
> A: How do I know you're really his brother?
> B: Are you serious right now? 你现在是认真的吗？ / 你没开玩笑吧？
> A: Yes. We've had strangers coming into the building lately. 是的。我们（这里）曾有过陌生人（闯入/出现）
> B: Okay, but you're treating me like a criminal. 好吧，但你现在把我当成罪犯一样对待了。
> A: I'm just being careful.
> B: Fine. I'll call Daniel.
> A: That's all I was asking you to do.
> B: Wait... he's calling me right now.
> A: Go ahead. Answer it.  **go ahead**：非常地道的高频口语短语，意为“请便、去吧、开始吧”。既可以用来表示礼貌地允许别人做某事（如 _Go ahead._），也可以带有挑衅意味地让对方“有本事就做”。
> B: Hello? Yeah. Daniel. I'm downstairs.
> A: Is he coming down? 他正要下来吗？
> B: Yes. He forgot to tell me the entry code.
> A: Okay. Then I'm sorry for stopping you.
> B: I understand why you did it, but you came at me pretty strongly. - **come at someone**：非常地道的口语短语，意为“向某人发难、气势汹汹地冲过来（指责或质问）”。- **pretty strongly**：意为“相当激烈地、挺厉害的”。_pretty_ 在口语中常作副词，意为“相当、很”。
> A: You came through the door without saying anything.
> B: Fair point. I should've explained first. **Fair point.**：极其地道的高频口语短语，意为“言之有理、这倒也是”。
> A: And I shouldn't have assumed the worst. **assume the worst**：固定短语，意为“把事情往最坏处想、做最坏的猜测”。形容在没弄清真相之前，盲目地预判对方有恶意或会出事。
> B: Looks like we both started this the wrong way.
> A: Agreed. Next time, just use the intercom.
> B: Trust me, after this conversation, I definitely will. 


## 10. Why Are You Late?
> A: Mark? Why are you so late?
> B: I'm really sorry. I came as fast as I could.
> A: Your interview was thirty minutes ago.
> B: I know. I didn't mean to keep you waiting. 我知道。我不是故意让你等的。
> A: You could have called us.
> B: I tried, but my phone died on the way. **phone died**：地道口语表达，意为“手机没电了 / 关机了”。
> A: Your phone died at exactly the wrong time? **at exactly the wrong time**：意为“偏偏在最糟糕、最不凑巧的时间”。用来形容倒霉或尴尬的时机。
> B: I know how that sounds, but it's true. 我知道这听起来（很不靠谱/像借口），但这是真的。      **I know how that sounds...**：地道的高频交际短语，意为“我知道这听起来可能……（不太好/像在编借口）”。通常用于当你试图解释一桩听起来很离谱、很难让人相信的事情之前，先表示理解对方的疑虑。
> A: We take being on time very seriously here. - **take something seriously**：高频经典固定搭配，意为“认真对待…… / 对……高度重视”。**being on time**：动名词短语作宾语，意为“守时、准时到达”。
> B: I understand. I usually arrive early.
> A: Then what happened today?
> B: There was an accident on the highway.
> A: An accident? Were you involved? **be involved (in something)**：常用固定搭配，意为“卷入……、牵涉其中、参与”。当询问某人是否和某件坏事或突发状况有直接关系时，经常使用这个句型。
> B: No, but traffic completely stopped. 
> A: You should have left home earlier.
> B: I left an hour before the interview. 
> A: And that still wasn't enough?
> B: Normally, it only takes twenty minutes to get here.
> A: Hmm... how long were you stuck? **be stuck**：固定常用搭配，意为“被困住、动弹不得、卡在某处”
> B: Almost forty minutes. Nobody was moving.
> A: Why didn't you take another road?
> B: I couldn't. We were trapped between cars. **be trapped between...**：意为“被困在……之间”。
> A: This is not a good start to an interview, Mark. **to an interview**：介词短语作后置定语，修饰 _start_，意为“对一场面试而言”。
> B: I know, and I'm embarrassed about it. **be embarrassed about something**：固定搭配，意为“对某事感到尴尬/羞愧”。
> A: I almost gave your interview time to someone else.  我差点把你的面试时间安排给别人了。
> B: I understand. I wouldn't blame you. **I wouldn't blame you**：地道交际短语，意为“我不会责怪你（做这个决定）的”。
> A: You're not going to make another excuse? **make an excuse**：固定搭配，意为“找借口、找理由”。
> B: No. I can only tell you what happened. 
> A: Hmm... at least you're being honest about it.
> B: I really want this opportunity. 
> A: Wanting the job and being responsible are different things. 想要这份工作和有责任心是两回事。也可以理解为：想得到这份工作，并不代表你就是一个负责任的人。
> B: You're right. I should have had a backup plan.
> A: Wait. Is that blood on your hand?
> B: Oh... it's just a small cut.
> A: How did that happen?
> B: A motorcyclist fell near my car.  有个骑摩托车的人在我的车旁摔倒了。
> A: I stopped to help him.
> B: You stopped to help someone?
> A: Yes. I couldn't leave him there.
> B: You should have told me that from the beginning.
> A: I didn't want to use it as an excuse.
> B: All right, Mark. Sit down. Let's start your interview.
> A: Thank you. I really appreciate that.


## 11. The Forgotten Money 
> A: Here you are, Ma'am. One cold coffee.
> B: Thank you. It looks perfect.
> A: I'm glad you like it. That will be two dollars, please.
> B: Two dollars... Oh, wait.
> A: Is everything alright? 
> B: I've just realized something terrible. I left my purse at home? **purse**：名词，意为“（女式）钱包、手提包”。
> A: You mean you have no money with you?
> B: Not even a single dollar. I'm really sorry.连一美元都没有 / 一分钱也没有。
> A: But your coffee has already been served.
> B: I know, and I feel awful about it. I honestly thought I had my purse with me.
> A: Could you pay with your phone?
> B: I wish I could, but my phone is almost dead and my payment app isn't working.
> A: Then I'm afraid I'll have to take the coffee back.
> B: Please, don't. I haven't even touched it yet.
> A: I understand, but I can't let you leave without paying.
> B: What if I leave my phone here and come back with the money?
> A: Your phone is worth far more than two dollars.
> B: Then I'll leave something else. I just don't want the coffee wasted.
> A: You seem very sure that you'll come back.
> B: Because I will. I live just around the corner.
> A: And what if you simply walk away? **And what if...?**：在对话中常用来承接上文、提出一个意想不到的假设或反问，意为“要是……会怎么样呢？
> B: I wouldn't do that. Look, I'll give you my name and address. 
> A: That won't be necessary. 
> B: Then what can I do?
> A: Take the coffee.
> B: Wait... what?
> A: Take it. Come back later and pay the two dollars.
> B: You're really trusting me? 
> A: I'm giving you one chance. Don't make me regret it. 
> B: I won't. I promise.
> A: Good. Now enjoy your coffee before it gets warm.
> B: Thank you. I'll be back before you even miss me. **before you even miss me**：极其生动且俏皮的口语表达，字面意思是“甚至在你开始想念我之前”
> A: I'll hold you to that. 我可就记住你的话了 / 一言为定哦。**hold someone to something**：地道的高频习惯用法，意为“要求某人兑现承诺、履行诺言”。
> B: You have my word. 我向你保证 / 我说话算话。
> A: Then we have a deal. **have a deal**：固定商务及口语搭配，意为“达成协议 / 成交 / 一言为定”。
